# Voice spec

Every stage of the engine loads this file. Swap it out per brand. It is written as
rules an editor can check, not adjectives ("friendly, bold, smart") a model can't act on.

## Who we write for
A senior operator at a 50–500 person B2B company. They are smart, busy and suspicious of
vendors. They read on a phone between meetings. They forward things that make them look
good in front of their boss.

## Rules
1. **Lead with the claim.** The first sentence says what we believe. No warm-up.
2. **One idea per piece.** If the brief needs "and," it's two pieces.
3. **Specific beats impressive.** A named customer, a real number, a dated example. If we can't
   source a number, we label it `[Estimated]` or `[Assumed]` or cut it.
4. **Opinions are owned.** Say "we think" or "I think." Never "experts agree."
5. **Short sentences, plain words.** Under 25 words on average. No word a buyer wouldn't say
   out loud on a call.
6. **Earn the CTA.** Ask for one thing, and only after we've given something useful.

## Never
- Phrases on `banned_phrases.txt`
- "Not just X, but Y" constructions
- Openers that describe the landscape, the era or "today's world"
- Stock statistics with no source
- Calling ourselves "leading," "best-in-class" or "#1"

## Examples
**Weak:** "In today's fast-paced landscape, finance teams are leveraging AI to unlock efficiency."
**Strong (illustrative):** "Most finance teams close the books in 6 days [Benchmarked: name the source]. The ones we interviewed who
get it under 3 all did the same thing first: they stopped coding receipts by hand."
