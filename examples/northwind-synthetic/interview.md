# SME interview: Northwind (synthetic example)

> **Synthetic data.** Northwind, its people and every competitor named in this example are
> fictional. This transcript was written to exercise the pipeline. It is not a real customer
> conversation.

**Company:** Northwind, accounts-payable automation for mid-market finance teams
**SME:** Dana Ortiz, Head of Customer Success (fictional)
**Interviewer:** Content lead
**Length:** 30 minutes, lightly edited

---

**Q: What do customers get wrong in the first 90 days?**

Dana: They automate the wrong invoices first. Everyone wants to start with the big, ugly
vendor invoices because that's where the pain feels worst. But those are the ones with
weird line items and exceptions. The teams that succeed start with the boring, high-volume
stuff: utilities, SaaS subscriptions, the same 40 vendors every month. You get the win fast,
the controller trusts the system, then you go after the hard ones.

**Q: How fast is "fast"?**

Dana: In our onboarding cohort last year, the teams that started with recurring vendors
hit their first fully touchless month about five weeks in. The ones that started with
complex invoices, we saw a lot of them stall for a quarter. I'd want to pull the exact
numbers before you publish that, though.

**Q: What does the buyer actually worry about? It's not the price, right?**

Dana: It's audit. Nobody says it in the first call, but the controller is thinking, "if I
let a machine approve this and it's wrong, it's my name on it." So the thing that closes
deals isn't the accuracy rate. It's showing them the audit trail. Who approved what, when,
and what rule it matched. When we started opening demos with the audit log instead of the
OCR, it changed the conversation.

**Q: What advice out there do you disagree with?**

Dana: "Get to 100% touchless." That's a bad goal. If you're at 100%, you're either lying or
you've stopped looking at exceptions. The healthy number is high, but there should always be
a pile a human looks at. The exceptions are where you catch fraud and duplicate billing.

**Q: Anything else?**

Dana: Month-end. Everyone talks about invoice processing time. Nobody talks about what it
does to close. When AP is clean on day one, the close gets shorter because you're not
chasing accruals. That's the story the CFO cares about, and almost nobody in our space tells it.
